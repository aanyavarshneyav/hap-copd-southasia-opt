# Critical Review of the Brief & Risk Register

This document audits methodological, computational, and data risks in the project brief and defines concrete mitigations before implementation starts.

| ID | Risk Area | Problem Identified in Brief | Severity | Concrete Fix / Mitigation | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `RSK-01` | **GA Fitness Evaluation Cost (CPU Laptop Constraint)** | Evaluating the full Stage 2 forecasting/ML/BAPC pipeline + uncertainty draws inside every NSGA-II evaluation (`pop=80 × gen=100 = 8,000+` calls) would take hours on CPU laptops. | **High** | Use a two-tier architecture: (1) Person A exports precomputed BAU projections (`forecasts_stage3.csv`) + a fast vectorized `exposure_response_surrogate.json` for millisecond fitness evaluation inside NSGA-II/MDP; (2) re-evaluate the final Pareto set on full Stage 2 uncertainty draws (`s3_pareto_reeval.csv`). | Person A + B |
| `RSK-02` | **Causal Intervention Effect vs. Correlational Forecast** | Observational forecasting models (ARIMA, ETS, RF/GBDT on 5 countries) capture historical associations, not necessarily causal clean-cooking intervention effects; GBD 2023 exposure/IER tables may be unexportable. | **High** | Use WHO SDG 7.1.2 clean-fuel access as the exposure driver (`DATA_SPEC.md` fallback), anchor exposure-response elasticities via published GBD/WHO literature in `ASSUMPTIONS.md`, bound all effects with sensitivity ranges, and strictly frame results as scenario analyses (never causal policy claims). | Person A + Shared |
| `RSK-03` | **Allocation Granularity & Urban/Rural Apportionment** | India and Nepal nationally are only 2 units (too small for meaningful spatial allocation), while sub-national Indian states were excluded to keep scope manageable. Splitting into 4 urban/rural units introduces apportionment uncertainty because GBD 2023 reports national (not urban/rural) COPD burden. | **High** | Split India and Nepal into 4 units (`IND_urban`, `IND_rural`, `NPL_urban`, `NPL_rural` = `48` GA vars across 4 interventions × 3 periods) using explicit population × solid-fuel exposure weighting (`ASM-01` in `ASSUMPTIONS.md`), and run sensitivity checks varying the assumed urban-to-rural conditional susceptibility ratio by $\pm 20\%$. | Person A |
| `RSK-04` | **Female–Male Equity Objective Definition** | "Minimise female–male burden gap" is ambiguous: absolute DALY difference (`\|DALYs_F - DALYs_M\|`) depends heavily on population size/age structure, whereas ASR gap (`\|ASR_F - ASR_M\|`) measures per-capita rate disparity. Also, clean-cooking rollout data rarely disaggregates adoption by sex. | **Medium** | Define primary objective as population-weighted absolute ASR gap $\sum_u p_u \|\text{ASR}^F_{u,2040} - \text{ASR}^M_{u,2040}\|$ (`ASM-03`), log sex-differential exposure elasticity explicitly (`ASM-04`), and run sensitivity against total absolute DALY gap. | Person B |
| `RSK-05` | **Small-Panel Overfitting & CV Leakage in Stage 2 ML** | The South Asia panel has only $5 \text{ countries} \times 34 \text{ years} = 170$ rows per sex. Complex tree ensembles (`RF`/`HistGBDT`) easily overfit collinear time trends or leak future data if random K-fold CV is used. | **High** | Enforce strict expanding-window panel rolling-origin CV (`origins: 2008, 2013, 2018`, horizon `5y`), restrict ML to shallow trees / strong regularization and low-dimensional domain features (`clean_fuel_share`, urban share, 65+ demographic share), and require beating simple statistical baselines (`ARIMA`, `ETS`, `Poisson APC`) on MASE. | Person A |
| `RSK-06` | **Unequal MDP vs. GA Comparison** | Comparing an adaptive sequential MDP against a static NSGA-II plan is invalid if they use different budgets, discount rates, action granularity, or response curves (especially since NSGA-II has 3 continuous objectives and MDP maximizes a scalarized reward). | **High** | Enforce strict parity: lock both to identical `baseline_unit_state`, `exposure_response_surrogate`, total budget cap, period costs, and adoption limits. Evaluate the GA open-loop schedule inside the exact same stochastic transition simulator as the MDP across matched scalarization weights $(\lambda_{\text{cost}}, \lambda_{\text{eq}})$ (`PLANNING_SPEC.md` §4). | Person B |
| `RSK-07` | **MDP State & Action Space Explosion** | Joint state over 4 continuous coverages + infra + budget easily explodes past $10^5\text{–}10^7$ states/actions, exceeding laptop RAM/time limits. | **Medium** | Discretize coverage into 5–6 coarse tiers (`20%` bins), track 2 rural infra flags and 6 budget tiers, restrict actions to $\le 50$ budget-feasible macro-bundles, and use sparse transition indexing in finite-horizon NumPy DP (`PLANNING_SPEC.md` §1.2). | Person B |
| `RSK-08` | **STRIPS-Only Limitation in `pyperplan`** | `pyperplan` does not support PDDL 2.1 numeric fluents or action costs (`(= (budget) ...)`), so continuous budgets cannot be encoded directly in PDDL. | **Medium** | Discretize budgets into consumable `budget-token` objects and coverage into discrete tiers (`cov-low`, `cov-med`, `cov-high`), keeping PDDL focused on verifying valid prerequisite/infrastructure action sequencing rather than numeric optimisation. | Person B |
| `RSK-09` | **GBD 2023 Pandemic Structural Shift (2020–2023)** | The final rolling-origin validation fold (`2019–2023`) spans COVID-19 years, where COPD mortality/DALY trends in GBD 2023 may exhibit structural breaks. | **Low–Med** | Report Fold 3 metrics both including `2020–2023` and up to `2019` (and check `pwlf` breakpoints around `2020`) so model selection isn't distorted by pandemic shock artifacts. | Person A |
